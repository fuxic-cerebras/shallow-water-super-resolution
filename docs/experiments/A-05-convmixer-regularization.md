# A-05 — ConvMixer regularization, and the training-curve comparison

Companion to the two figures in `viz/`, and the write-up of record for A-05. Every number is
read from the run artifacts, nothing recomputed:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=$PWD python scripts/plot_comparison.py --name compare_architectures \
    --title "Architecture comparison" \
    --runs runs/<unet> runs/<edsr> runs/<convmixer>

PYTHONPATH=$PWD python scripts/plot_comparison.py --name compare_convmixer_variants \
    --title "ConvMixer variants" \
    --runs runs/<convmixer> runs/<droppath> runs/<wd> runs/<d12> runs/<nonorm>
```

`docs/RESULTS.md` remains the headline artifact and is generated by `swe_sr.report`; this file
is hand-written and adds the training dynamics that the generated table does not carry.

Column notes. `best val` is the checkpoint-selection metric, lowest macro-averaged normalized
MSE on the full validation split, and `best ep` is where it occurred — not always the last
epoch, because several arms early-stop. `gap` is `best val` over the training loss *at that
same epoch*, so it measures generalization at the selected checkpoint rather than at the end of
a run. `test` is the held-out split with a 95% trajectory bootstrap interval. For scale, bicubic
scores 0.4295 on the same split and predicting the channel mean scores exactly 1.0.

## 1. Architectures — `viz/compare_architectures.png`

U-Net, EDSR, and ConvMixer as published (D023), on the frozen manifest, seed 20260812, and an
identical 30,000-step schedule.

The figure shows what the aggregate hides: **ConvMixer reaches the lowest training loss of the
three** (0.0103, below the U-Net's 0.0145) and still finishes between them on validation. It is
the only arm to early-stop, peaking at epoch 21 and then overfitting for fifteen epochs. EDSR
is the opposite failure — a 1.26x gap means it never fit the training data at all.

Error by lead time, which is where the architectural hypothesis is actually tested:

<!-- BEGIN generated: results:architectures-lead-time -->
| model | <= 12 h | 16-24 h | > 24 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| U-Net | **0.0178** | 0.0371 | **0.0666** |
| ConvMixer | 0.0341 | **0.0359** | 0.1245 |
| EDSR | 0.0765 | 0.0496 | 0.1229 |
<!-- END generated: results:architectures-lead-time -->

D023 predicted that an isotropic 129 px receptive field would pay off where basin-scale
structure dominates, and in the 16–24 h band it does: ConvMixer is the best of the three there,
reaching 0.78x the U-Net's error at 18.8 h. The deficit is at **short** lead times, where the
correct output is nearly bicubic — between 3.7 and 4.7 h ConvMixer is at or worse than bicubic
(0.0483 against 0.0402 at 4.7 h) while the U-Net beats bicubic everywhere. Receptive field is
this architecture's strength here, not its limitation, which is why the A-05 arms below leave
kernel and patch size alone.

## 2. ConvMixer variants — `viz/compare_convmixer_variants.png`

A-05 (regularization) and A-03 (normalization). Each arm varies one factor against the
published run, except the unnormalized one — see `docs/experiments/A-03-convmixer-normalization.md` for why that
one cannot.

Those intervals overlap heavily, because trajectory-to-trajectory spread dominates. The paired
test is the one that carries the verdict — paired on trajectory, negative favours the arm:

<!-- BEGIN generated: results:convmixer-paired -->
| arm | paired diff | 95% CI | excludes 0 | arm better on |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| + stochastic depth 0.1 | **-0.00561** | [-0.00873, -0.00247] | **yes** | 6 of 8 |
| 256/12 (depth 16 -> 12) | -0.00179 | [-0.00713, +0.00180] | no | 5 of 8 |
| + weight decay 1e-2 | -0.00062 | [-0.00330, +0.00114] | no | 2 of 8 |
| no normalization (A-03) | +0.04692 | [+0.01895, +0.07052] | yes, *worse* | 1 of 8 |
<!-- END generated: results:convmixer-paired -->

These intervals are the **percentile bootstrap over trajectories** that `docs/VALIDATION.md`
specifies, seed 0. They replace the paired t-intervals this table carried until the numbers
became generated: `mean +/- t_{7,0.975} * SE`, which is symmetric by construction and on these
eight pairs runs 10-20% wider (stochastic depth read `[-0.00968, -0.00153]`). Every mean
difference, verdict, and win count is unchanged — the two estimators disagree only on width —
but only one of them is the protocol the rest of the project uses, and a hand-computed interval
is exactly what a generated table exists to prevent.

**Only stochastic depth is a resolvable improvement**: 8.6% better, and it fixed the
overfitting it targeted — the gap falls 3.61x -> 2.48x, now tighter than the U-Net's 2.73x, and
the arm uses its whole budget instead of peaking at epoch 21. It adds no parameters.

**Weight decay did nothing, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a property of the model.**
AdamW's decoupled update shrinks by `lr * wd` per step, so at `lr = 1e-4` a decay of 1e-2 is a
per-step factor of 1e-6, and cosine decay drives even that to zero. The setting needs to be
roughly 100x larger before it regularizes anything. The arm tests a badly chosen value, not the
lever; A-06 should re-run it at 1e0 or above.

**Cutting capacity failed outright.** The gap stayed at 5.09x, so depth 12 removed parameters
without removing overfitting. Together with stochastic depth succeeding, that points at a
missing *noise* regularizer rather than excess parameter count. It is still the efficiency
result of the set: it matches the published arm at 20% fewer parameters and 1.65x the
throughput (40.1 against 24.3 samples/s).

Error by lead time, which is where this gets interesting:

<!-- BEGIN generated: results:convmixer-lead-time -->
| arm | <= 12 h | 16-24 h | > 24 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| + stochastic depth 0.1 | 0.0367 | 0.0382 | **0.0984** |
| 256/12 (depth 16 -> 12) | 0.0343 | 0.0356 | 0.1199 |
| + weight decay 1e-2 | 0.0342 | **0.0331** | 0.1253 |
| published | **0.0341** | 0.0359 | 0.1245 |
| no normalization (A-03) | 0.1258 | 0.0469 | 0.1703 |
<!-- END generated: results:convmixer-lead-time -->

**The entire stochastic-depth gain is at long lead times** (-21% above 24 h); below 12 h it is
slightly *worse*. And the short-lead-time deficit against the U-Net's 0.0178 survives all three
regularization arms essentially unchanged, at 0.0341–0.0367.

That is the most useful finding here. Regularization was the right diagnosis for the
overfitting, but the overfitting was never the cause of the short-lead-time failure. A deficit
that is untouched by weight decay, stochastic depth, and capacity reduction alike is
structural, and the structural difference is that the U-Net refines on the bicubic-upsampled
128x128 grid with encoder skips, so a near-identity correction is easy for it, while ConvMixer
must reconstruct one from a 32x32 representation through a 256 -> 64 projection with no skip
path. `TASKS.md` A-08 carries that experiment.

## Caveats

- `drop_path = 0.1` is a conventional starting value, untuned, and its best epoch was 27 of 39,
  so the arm had not saturated. A sweep, or a longer budget, may yield more.
- The 256/12 arm sits below EDSR's parameter count and so outside the capacity-ordered band
  D023 fixed for the published three-way comparison.
- Both figures are light-mode only, matching every other figure here. The palette is checked for
  colour-vision separation rather than chosen by eye; the previous tab10 palette failed twice
  (see the `scripts/plot_comparison.py` docstring), so figures regenerated after this change
  will differ in colour from any older copy.
- All ConvMixer runs pass `scripts/verify_independent.py`, which recomputes every metric in
  plain numpy from the stored arrays.
